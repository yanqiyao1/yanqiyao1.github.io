About

I am a final-year undergraduate in Computer Science at Peking University. I will join the Institute of Computational Linguistics at PKU as a Ph.D. student in 2027, advised by Prof. Houfeng Wang.

My research aims to understand how large language models actually reason — what happens inside the network between reading a problem and emitting an answer — and to make that reasoning more reliable. Concretely, I work on mechanistic interpretability of logical reasoning, diagnosis and calibration of model outputs, and cost-aware test-time decision making for agents.

Publications

Wrong Prediction, Right Answer: Recovering Evidence from Collapsed LLM Sequence Scores

Qiyao Yan EMNLP 2026 · Main First author

We study cases where a model internally retains evidence for the correct answer yet outputs the wrong one, disentangling reasoning failure from output-scoring distortion. A lightweight, label-free correction using only 25 unlabeled samples recovers 9–34 accuracy points across four logical-reasoning benchmarks.

Towards a Mechanistic Understanding of Propositional Logical Reasoning in Large Language Models

Qiyao Yan et al. EMNLP 2026 · Main Co-first author

Through causal interventions on a controlled benchmark of 20,000 paired samples per prompt order, we identify four cross-model reasoning patterns: staged computation, boundary-position information aggregation, persistent backtracking to factual premises, and functionally specialized attention heads.

Selected Project

MetaBudget: Think, Ask, or Answer

Independent research project · Ongoing · Targeting ICLR

A unified in-turn metareasoning framework in which a language model makes cost-sensitive choices among internal reasoning (think), external clarification (ask), and immediate answering (answer). We formalize the competition between the value of computation and the value of information under test-time costs, and train cost-conditioned policies (SFT cold-start + cost-conditioned GRPO) to learn when asking beats thinking.
